Explanation

Red light therapy has gained popularity for skin rejuvenation, pain relief, wound healing and more. One key variable that affects results is the red light therapy distance from body. Too close or too far can impact how much light energy (photons) your tissues absorb. This guide compares treatment distances of 6 inches and 24 inches, explains pros and cons of each, and helps you choose the best setup for your needs.

Why Distance Matters

Red light therapy devices deliver specific wavelengths (typically 630–660 nm and 810–850 nm) at a certain power density (measured in milliwatts per square centimeter, mW/cm²). The intensity you receive decreases as distance increases. Key points:

Inverse-square law: Light intensity drops roughly with the square of the distance. Doubling the distance cuts intensity to about 25%.

Tissue penetration: Higher intensity at closer range can penetrate more deeply, but heat and safety must be considered.

Treatment area: Closer distances concentrate on a smaller area; farther distances cover more skin but with lower intensity.

Understanding these factors helps you balance comfort, safety and effectiveness.

Benefits

Considerations

Higher skin heating; watch for discomfort or redness.

Smaller treatment zone per session; you may need to move device around.

Risk of eye strain or damage if proper eyewear isn't used.

Practical Tips for Effective Sessions

Protect Your EyesAlways wear FDA-compliant goggles or protective glasses.

Maintain Consistent DistanceUse a spacer, ruler or built-in device stand to hold distance steady.

Track Session ParametersLog distance, duration and any effects (e.g., pain relief, skin glow).

Avoid OveruseTypical protocols are 2–5 sessions per week. Follow device guidelines.

Stay HydratedDrinking water supports the body's natural healing response.

Combine TherapiesPair red light with stretching or topical treatments for synergistic effects.

Mind SafetyDo not use on areas with active bleeding, open cancerous lesions or over thyroid gland without supervision.

When to Seek Medical Advice

Red light therapy is generally safe, but it's not a substitute for professional diagnosis or treatment of serious conditions. If you experience any concerning symptoms—severe pain, uncontrolled inflammation, unexpected skin reactions—stop therapy and speak to a doctor immediately.
