Erectile dysfunction (ED) is common. It affects millions of men and becomes more likely with age, stress, certain medications, and health conditions like diabetes or heart disease. While prescription medications such as PDE5 inhibitors (like sildenafil) are often effective, they're not the only option.

For many couples, learning how to use toys to help his ED can reduce pressure, improve intimacy, and restore satisfying sex. The right tools can support erections, enhance stimulation, and shift the focus from "performance" to pleasure and connection.

This guide explains practical, evidence-based options and how to use them safely and effectively.

First: Make Sure the Basics Are Covered

Before adding toys, it's important to understand what may be contributing to ED.

Erectile dysfunction can be:

Physical (blood flow problems, nerve issues, hormone imbalances)

Psychological (stress, anxiety, depression)

Medication-related

Or a combination of these factors

Because ED can sometimes signal underlying cardiovascular disease or other serious health issues, it's worth taking a quick free symptom assessment to help identify what might be causing your symptoms and whether you should see a doctor right away.

And most importantly:If ED is new, worsening, or accompanied by chest pain, shortness of breath, or other concerning symptoms, speak to a doctor promptly.

When Toys Aren't Enough

Toys can support erections, but they don't treat underlying disease.

See a doctor if:

ED is persistent for 3+ months

You have diabetes, high blood pressure, or heart disease

Erections are painful

There is penile curvature or deformity

You experience low libido or other hormonal symptoms

Medical treatments may include:

Oral medications

Testosterone evaluation

Injections

Shockwave therapy

Surgical implants (in severe cases)

ED can sometimes be an early warning sign of cardiovascular disease. Do not ignore it.
