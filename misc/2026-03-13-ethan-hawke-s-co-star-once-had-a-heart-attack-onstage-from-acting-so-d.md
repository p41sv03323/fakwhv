It took like six cups of coffee and an actual, literal slap to the face to motivate me to finish this post, which is roughly 400 words long. Such is life. Human beings, naturally slovenly beasts, find it difficult to maintain dedication to their chosen profession from time to time.

Unless you are Canadian actor Richard Easton, that is, who straight up died on a Broadway stage and then woke up demanding his understudy give him the damn role back posthaste.

Easton's co-star, Ethan Hawke, relayed this story of Broadway badassery during a recent appearance on The Graham Norton Show. The incident, according to Hawke, occurred during a scene in Tom Stoppard's The Coast of Utopia, in which Easton's character gets worked into a rage.

"He was getting particularly mad. Particularly heated. And he had a heart attack and fell down on center stage," Hawke recalled. "He hit like a watermelon hit."

Now, to be very clear, I'm not recommending you follow this post-heart attack track. Do not immediately hop out of your hospital bed, possibly still wearing a gown, and go on to perform several nights of intensive theater work that Ethan Hawke will later describe on The Graham Norton Show as "some of the best shows of my life."

That's not healthy. It is very badass. But it is not healthy.
