Nothing pleases my little brother more than beating me in all types of competitions, even now as we're both getting older (he's 46, 11 years younger than me). He used to teach rock climbing, something I know very little about, but now he’s a chef. Last Thanksgiving we had one of our usual disagreements as he was cooking dinner, and he challenged me to open a jar of marinade. I thought I could do it easily, but I couldn’t open it without a rubber grip. He popped open another jar of the same marinade with ease. Of course, I’ll never live that down, but it seemed as though his rock climbing experience gave him the upper hand by improving his grip strength.

Having a strong grip is good for more than just opening jars or besting your big brother. Grip strength is considered a biomarker for overall strength, as it reflects overall activity and total body muscle development. Also, lack of grip strength can be an indicator of overall health and mortality, so it may be something to be concerned about when you can’t open bottle tops. For older guys, maintaining a strong grip is even more important. Most men experience declines in grip strength around age 55, according to Harvard Health Publishing. The decline could be associated with sarcopenia, which is natural, age-related decline in muscle mass. To stay healthy, older guys should continue to train their forearms.

One exercise my brother showed me to build his grip and arm strength for rock climbing is simply holding a weighted object and twisting it. All he did was take a frying pan and grip the handle. He then held it up with his forearm parallel to the floor and his upper arm pinned to the side of his body. He then rotated the frying pan from side to side by twisting his hand.

It was basically the same twisting motion you would use to twist a doorknob. I tried it and that action worked the muscles in my fingers, wrists, forearms, and upper arms. You can do it holding any object that places more of a load toward your index finger and away from your pinkie, such as holding a bar on its end.

Of course, I had to share an exercise of my own. You can also introduce that twisting motion to your arm curls using dumbbells. To start, stand up tall holding dumbbells at your side in a neutral position (palms facing each other). Grip the handles as hard as you can throughout the movement. Perform a standard curl, then pause the rep during eccentric part of the movement so your forearms are parallel to the floor with your palms facing the ceiling. From this position, twist your hands and forearms as you did for the previous exercise. Although the weight is not offset toward your forefinger in this case, you will still work hard to stabilize your arms as you’re twisting the dumbbells.
