# These New Heart Meds Could Be Game Changers. Here's How to Know If They're Right for You.

NO MATTER HOW diligent you are about regular workouts and smart food choices, bad genes can overpower even the healthiest lifestyle, making heart medication essential. Though many people who come to my cardiology office hate the idea of taking meds, several new drug classes are revolutionizing heart disease prevention by offering safe, highly effective ways to reduce risk.

New Talent

GLP-1 Agonists

You might know these drugs, originally developed for diabetes, as the most effective weight-loss drugs ever tested. Now, a growing number of studies show a positive impact on heart disease.

Here’s why: Fat tissue drives inflammation, which promotes plaque buildup in arteries. As a result, reducing fat stores can lower the risk of heart attacks and strokes. In a large clinical trial of overweight or obese people without diabetes but with a prior heart attack or stroke, semaglutide (Wegovy) lowered the risk of cardiovascular death, heart attack, and stroke by 20 percent.

SEVERAL EMERGING DRUGS show tremendous promise. They share a new mechanism: targeting messenger RNA (mRNA) inside cells to block production of specific proteins. Most current medications bind to proteins after they’re released from cells, but these work upstream of that.

Leqvio (Inclisiran)

This drug halts the production of a protein, known as PCSK9, that regulates cholesterol receptors on liver cells. By increasing the number of these receptors, Leqvio helps the liver break down cholesterol from the blood. In just two doses per year, this medication provides highly significant (greater than 70 percent) and sustained reductions in LDL cholesterol. Leqvio is a great option for people who need to lower their cholesterol but can’t reach their goal with statins.

Lp(a) Inhibitors

An elevated level of a molecule called lipoprotein(a), better known as Lp(a), is a risk factor for heart disease. Lp(a) levels are genetically determined and stable throughout life—unaffected by diet, exercise, or even statins. Yet cutting-edge medications such as lepodisiran and olpasiran interfere with the messengers that tell the body to produce Lp(a) and have been found to lower it by more than 95 percent. These are still only available in clinical trials.

Zilebesiran

Nearly half of American adults suffer from hypertension, and many require daily pills. Zilebesiran can take blood pressure down with just one or two treatments per year. Some traditional blood pressure meds block a hormone known as angiotensin, but this drug keeps your body from producing it. This is also research-only for now.
