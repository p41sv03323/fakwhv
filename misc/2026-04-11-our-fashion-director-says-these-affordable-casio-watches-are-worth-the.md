Casio’s funky little digital watches have gone unchanged since the early 1990s, and they have developed a cult following among watch enthusiasts. You could argue that the best Casio watch is the F-91W. Since it was released in 1989, it has gone on to sell more units than any other ticker in history. However, that would be overlooking all the updated variations that have come out since. Meanwhile, Casio’s its G-Shock line—which exploded and peaked in the early aughts—has undergone a renaissance in recent years among professional athletes. Plus, active guys get a whole other dimension of use from Casio’s sport watches. Few other brands offer so many training features on an every-day watch, especially in the sub-$300 range.

What to Consider

Case Size

How a watch case looks and feels depends on the size of your wrist. The measurement listed for case size is the horizontal width of a watch, not including the crown. Most watches run from 38 to 44 millimeters, but sport watches like G Shock can get even bigger. To get a feel for how a watch will look on your wrist, hold a ruler or tape measure over your wrist, use the centimeters side and just divide the listed case size by ten. It mostly comes down to preference, but a watch case should always be at least a few millimeters less than the width of your wrist.

Case thickness is just as important to how a watch feels on your wrist. This measurement is often buried deeper in the specs, but it is something you should look for when shopping online. G-Shock watches can get very thick, like 12 millimeters and up, while Casio watches are generally less than 10 millimeters thick.

Water Resistance

Casio watches are designed to handle the wear and tear of everyday life, and G-Shocks are built to handle everything you could possibly throw at them. However, water resistance is one of the most misleading and difficult to understand metrics in timekeeping.

To avoid getting into the weeds of what ATM actually means and why the meters measurement doesn’t really make sense, we provided a practical use for each watch. Water resistance is broken down by the most each watch can handle, ranging from some light splashing while washing your hands, through gentle submerging, surface swimming, up to actual SCUBA diving.

Band

Along with case size, band material and adjustability is the most important factor in how comfortable a watch will feel. Steel bracelets look nice for everyday wear but can snag, scratch your skin, and are vulnerable to extreme temperatures. Many Casio watches, especially the G-Shock line, use resin bands because they are lightweight, soft, and temperature neutral. The two drawbacks of resin bands are that they don’t look as stylish, and salt from sweat or the ocean can cause chaffing.

How do you improve upon the best selling watch of all time? Casio upgraded the F-91W by making the case and band out of stainless steel. This gave it a more refined look to a remarkably simple and easy-to-use digital watch. The case is small at 33 mm and only 9mm thick. It felt more a bracelet than a watch when it was on our wrist, which is the kind of minimal impact you'll never get tired of wearing. It is a true every-day watch with a unique-but-versatile appearance that our style editors think blends well with just about any outfit.

Testing out a bunch of digital watches came with some required instruction manual reading, but not in this case. Our testers found that the buttons were clearly marked and the interface was intuitive. Time adjustment, a stopwatch, and an alarm clock were all easy to find and use by clicking a couple buttons. The backlight button for the face is easy to access and quickly replaced reaching for our phone to check the time in the dark.

In fact, once our testers started using the alarm feature to wake up in the morning, they found that it broke the habit of spending time on their phones as soon as they woke up. We found that the super simple tech in this old school smart watch is a helpful and affordable way to cut back on screen time.

How to Wear Casio Watches

Casio watches may not look like any other every-day watch out there, but they are built to be worn all the time. The trick is that almost every watch the brand offers is monotone—usually black, silver, or gold with detail on the face—which makes for easy styling. Even when the watch has a retro appearance up close, if it appears to be a solid black band from five feet away, then you can wear it with practically anything.

Then there is the functionality aspect. Casio watches are built to make your life easier with tools like a stopwatch, alarm, and calendar. Long before smartwatches and smartphones, Casio made digital assistants that fit on your wrist. So you should take full advantage of those tools by wearing your Casio watch every day. You may even cut back on your screen time in the process.
