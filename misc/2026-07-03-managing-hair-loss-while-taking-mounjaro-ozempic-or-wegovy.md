Since these nutrients are absorbed through food, people who eat less or experience digestive issues while on these medications may not be absorbing enough nutrients to keep their hair healthy.

Get More Protein

One of the most important things you can do is get enough protein. Hair is made mostly of protein, so if your intake is too low, your body may slow down hair growth to focus on other, more important functions. Try to include lean meats, eggs, beans, nuts, and dairy in your diet. It is also important to eat a balanced diet with lots of vitamins and minerals, including iron, zinc, and biotin, which all play a role in keeping hair strong.

Take Hair-Strengthening Supplements

If you are struggling to get enough nutrients from food alone, your doctor may recommend supplements like biotin, iron, or omega-3s to help with your hair health. However, taking too much of certain vitamins, especially iron, can be harmful, so it is always best to check with a doctor first.

Another factor to consider is nutrient absorption. When you eat fewer calories, your body may not be absorbing enough nutrients to keep your hair healthy. B vitamins, omega-3 fatty acids, and zinc are all essential for keeping your scalp and hair follicles healthy, but they can be harder to get if you are eating less overall. Including a variety of nutrient-rich foods like leafy greens, fish, eggs, and nuts can help reduce hair loss and encourage new hair growth.

If you start noticing excessive shedding, it is a good idea to talk to your doctor.
