What Are Exosomes?

Exosomes are nano-sized extracellular vesicles (typically 30–150 nanometers in diameter) secreted by virtually all cell types, particularly stem cells. These vesicles carry a bioactive cargo that includes proteins, lipids, growth factors, and microRNA (miRNA)—all of which play crucial roles in cellular repair and intercellular communication.

In the body, exosomes act like molecular delivery vehicles. They travel between cells, transferring their contents to regulate inflammation, repair tissue damage, and modulate immune responses. Studies have shown that exosomes derived from mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) can influence angiogenesis (formation of new blood vessels), support wound healing, and promote anti-inflammatory activity.[1]

How Exosome IV Therapy Works

Exosome IV therapy begins with the collection and purification of exosomes from carefully screened donor stem cells, often derived from mesenchymal stem cells of umbilical cord tissue. These exosomes are processed under stringent laboratory conditions to ensure they are cell-free, sterile, and bioactive.

During an IV therapy session, the purified exosomes are suspended in saline and infused directly into the bloodstream. Once circulating, they seek out areas of inflammation or cellular damage. There, they deliver their regenerative payload—stimulating repair, reducing immune overactivation, and promoting tissue health. Exosomes are different from traditional stem cell therapy in that they are not whole cells.
