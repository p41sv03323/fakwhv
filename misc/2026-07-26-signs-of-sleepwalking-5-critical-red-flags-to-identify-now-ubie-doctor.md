What are the signs that someone may be sleepwalking?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sleepwalking is a sleep disorder in which a person walks or performs activities while still asleep. Common signs of sleepwalking include:

Walking or moving around while asleep

Sitting up in bed with eyes open but a blank, glassy expression

Being difficult to wake or confused when awakened

Not responding or responding slowly to conversation

Performing routine tasks (like getting dressed) unconsciously

Having no memory of the event the next morning

Sleepwalking most often occurs during deep non-REM sleep, typically within the first few hours after falling asleep. While occasional episodes are usually harmless, frequent sleepwalking may signal an underlying issue such as stress, sleep deprivation, or another sleep disorder.

[Sleepwalking Critical](https://copperglow.dns0663.com/blog/9149110606.html)

Sleepwalking, also known as somnambulism, is a condition where a person walks or performs other activities while not fully awake. Signs of sleepwalking include:

Walking during sleep: The person may get out of bed and walk around as if awake.

Blank expression: They often have a glassy-eyed stare or blank expression while moving.

Lack of response: If spoken to, the person may not respond or may respond slowly.

[Signs of Sleepwalking](https://cortexcompass.jcesqc8.com/blog/5569287438.html)

Confusion upon waking: If woken up, they might be confused or disoriented.

No memory of the event: Typically, the sleepwalker does not remember the sleepwalking episode the next day.

Sleepwalking is more common in children and can be triggered by factors like lack of sleep, stress, or illness. It's important to ensure the sleepwalker's safety by removing obstacles and securing doors and windows.
