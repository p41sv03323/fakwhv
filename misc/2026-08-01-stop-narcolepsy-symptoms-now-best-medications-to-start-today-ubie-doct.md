Narcolepsy is a chronic neurological disorder, but its symptoms can be effectively managed with medication. Prescription treatments are available to address both excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) and cataplexy—the two most disruptive symptoms of narcolepsy.

Common medications include stimulants and wake-promoting agents (like modafinil and armodafinil) to reduce daytime drowsiness, sodium oxybate to help with both cataplexy and nighttime sleep quality, and certain antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, and tricyclics) to control cataplexy episodes. Newer options like pitolisant and solriamfetol are also improving outcomes for many patients.

Because narcolepsy shares symptoms with other conditions—such as sleep apnea, depression, or thyroid disorders—an accurate diagnosis is essential before starting treatment. If you're experiencing persistent sleepiness, sudden muscle weakness, or sleep disturbances, understanding the potential cause is the critical first step. These help reduce daytime sleepiness. Other medicines, like sodium oxybate, can help with both sleepiness and cataplexy, which is when someone suddenly loses muscle strength.
