Cirrhosis is not caused by alcohol alone. In fact, many cases develop in people who don't drink. Common non-alcoholic causes include nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), chronic hepatitis B and C infections, autoimmune hepatitis, genetic disorders like hemochromatosis and Wilson's disease, bile duct diseases, and certain medications. Because early liver damage often has subtle or no symptoms, identifying the underlying cause early is critical to slowing or preventing further scarring.

Explanation

Cirrhosis is a condition where the liver becomes scarred and doesn't work well. While drinking too much alcohol is a common cause, there are other reasons someone might get cirrhosis.

Causes of Cirrhosis

Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (NAFLD): This happens when fat builds up in the liver and can lead to cirrhosis. It's not caused by alcohol, but rather by factors like being overweight or having diabetes.

Other Health Conditions: Some diseases, like hepatitis, can also cause cirrhosis. Hepatitis is an infection that harms the liver.

Genetic Disorders: Some people are born with conditions that make cirrhosis more likely, like Wilson's disease or hemochromatosis, which affect how the body handles certain minerals.
