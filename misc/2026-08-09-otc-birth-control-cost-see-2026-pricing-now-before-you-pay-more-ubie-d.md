Over-the-counter (OTC) birth control is designed to be more accessible and affordable than prescription options, though costs can vary by product and pharmacy.

How much does OTC birth control cost?

Opill (daily progestin-only pill): Roughly $20 for a one-month supply, or about $50 for a three-month supply.

Condoms: Typically $0.50–$2 each, depending on brand and quantity.

Spermicides and contraceptive sponges: Generally $10–$20 per package.

Emergency contraception (e.g., Plan B): Around $40–$50 per dose, with generic versions often costing less.

Why OTC options are more affordable:

No doctor's visit or prescription required

Available at most pharmacies, supermarkets, and online retailers

Over-the-counter (OTC) birth control is designed to be easier for people to get without needing a prescription. According to recent studies, making birth control available OTC can help reduce barriers and make it more accessible for many people.

Cost Considerations

Affordability: OTC birth control is often priced to be more affordable than prescription options. However, the exact cost can vary depending on the brand and type of contraception.

Accessibility: By removing the need for a doctor's visit, OTC options can save on additional healthcare costs, making them a more cost-effective choice for many.
