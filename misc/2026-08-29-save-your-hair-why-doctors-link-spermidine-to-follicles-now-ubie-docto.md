How Hair Follicles Work

Hair grows from follicles located in the dermis. Each follicle cycles through three phases:

Anagen (growth phase)

Catagen (transition phase)

Telogen (resting phase)

Healthy follicles spend more time in anagen, producing thicker, longer hair. Disruptions—due to age, hormones, stress, or nutrient deficiencies—can shorten anagen and prolong telogen, causing thinning.

Spermidine's Role in Hair Follicle Health

Researchers believe spermidine may benefit hair follicles through several mechanisms:

Promoting AutophagyAutophagy clears damaged cellular components, keeping follicle cells healthy. With age, natural autophagy slows. Spermidine can reactivate this recycling process, potentially revitalizing aging hair follicles.

Stimulating Cell ProliferationBy supporting cell division, spermidine helps maintain a robust population of follicle cells. More active cells can extend the anagen phase, leading to increased hair thickness.

Clinical Evidence for Spermidine and Hair Thickness

While human studies are limited, preliminary research offers promising insights:

A 2020 pilot study published in Dermato-Endocrinology found that topical spermidine improved hair density and shine in participants with mild hair loss over a 6-month period.

Animal studies demonstrate that spermidine supplementation increases hair shaft thickness and prolongs anagen in mice.

Laboratory experiments on isolated human hair follicles show enhanced growth and delayed entry into telogen after spermidine treatment.

Together, these findings support the concept of spermidine for hair thickness, though larger, controlled trials are needed to confirm optimal dosages and long-term safety.
