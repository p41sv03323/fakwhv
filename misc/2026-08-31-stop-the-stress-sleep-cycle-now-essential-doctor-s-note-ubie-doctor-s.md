Published on: 7/21/2026

Why does stress make it hard to sleep, and lack of sleep increase stress?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Stress and sleep are closely connected in a two-way cycle: stress makes it harder to fall and stay asleep, and poor sleep increases stress hormones like cortisol, leaving you feeling more anxious the next day. This loop can be difficult to break without identifying what's driving it.

Common signs stress is affecting your sleep include racing thoughts at bedtime, waking during the night, early morning awakenings, and daytime fatigue or irritability. Underlying issues—such as anxiety disorders, insomnia, or hormonal imbalances—can also play a role.

Because the causes vary from person to person, understanding your specific symptoms is the first step toward relief. Stress and sleep are closely connected and can affect each other in a cycle. When you are stressed, your body releases chemicals that can make it hard to relax and fall asleep. This is because stress can make your brain very active, which keeps you awake at night.

When you don't get enough sleep, it can make you feel more stressed. Lack of sleep can make it harder for your body to handle stress because it can affect how your brain works, especially the parts that help you deal with stress.
