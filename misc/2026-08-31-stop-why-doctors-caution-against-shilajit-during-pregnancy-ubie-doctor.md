Explanation

Shilajit is a dark, tar-like substance used in Ayurvedic medicine for centuries. Rich in minerals and fulvic acid, it's touted for boosting energy, enhancing nutrient absorption, and supporting overall wellness. However, when it comes to pregnancy safety, doctors urge caution. This article explores what shilajit is, why medical professionals advise against its use during pregnancy, and safer alternatives for expecting mothers.

Why Pregnancy Changes the Equation

During pregnancy, both maternal and fetal health must be protected. The placenta filters many substances, but not all. What crosses to the developing baby can have lasting consequences. Below are the main reasons doctors caution against shilajit in pregnancy:

Risk of Contaminants

Heavy metals (lead, mercury, arsenic): Natural sources of shilajit may contain unsafe levels.

Mycotoxins and bacteria: Poorly processed products can harbor molds or pathogens.

Lack of standardization: Supplements vary widely in purity and potency.

Lack of Safety Data

No clinical trials assess shilajit's effects on pregnant women or developing fetuses.

Animal studies and anecdotal reports can't replace rigorous human research.

Potential Hormonal Interactions

Shilajit may influence testosterone, estrogen, or other hormone levels.

Altered hormone balance can affect fetal development and pregnancy progression.

Drug Interactions

Prenatal vitamins and medications (e.g., blood thinners, iron supplements) may interact unpredictably with shilajit.

Risk of reduced efficacy or increased side effects.

Unknown Long-Term Effects

Even if short-term tolerance seems fine, the lifelong impact on a child's health is unstudied.

What Major Health Authorities Say

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) does not approve shilajit as a drug or prenatal supplement.

The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) advises pregnant women to avoid non-standardized herbal remedies without proven safety profiles.

World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines emphasize caution with traditional medicines during pregnancy unless benefits clearly outweigh risks.

Safe Practices for Supplement Use in Pregnancy

To support your health and your baby's development, consider these guidelines:

Always discuss new supplements with your obstetrician or midwife before starting them.
