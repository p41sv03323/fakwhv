Safety

Winner: Life Extension

A thorough evaluation of dozens of lysine studies found a safe upper intake limit for supplemental lysine to be around 6g per day.5 And when adverse reactions occurred in trials, they were not too serious. Typical side effects included gastrointestinal symptoms like nausea, stomach ache, and diarrhea.

Despite the mild nature of these side effects, the risk of adverse reactions appears to be dose-dependent, so taking more lysine will increase those odds, especially if you already get a lot of lysine from your diet. Many lysine supplements provide you with 1,000mg or 500mg pills. We’ve seen doses closer to 500mg underperform in certain studies, so Life Extension’s 620mg dose might be slightly more effective without sacrificing as much safety as a jump to 1,000mg might.6

What is lysine?

Lysine is an essential amino acid that helps regulate muscle protein synthesis and plays a role in numerous other vital processes.8 9 Amino acids are commonly called the building blocks of proteins, and our bodies produce many of them internally. But our bodies can’t create essential amino acids like lysine, so we need to attain them through diet or supplementation for proper functioning.29

Research has shown that lysine also has some anti-inflammatory and anticonvulsant effects.10 11 It may boost immunity and aid in treating wounds, cold sores, and genital herpes, as well.12 13

How do lysine supplements work?

The body metabolizes lysine supplements in much the same way it does lysine derived from food. Since the body doesn’t make lysine on its own, it’s used to processing exogenous (outside) sources. Once consumed, lysine is primarily metabolized in the liver, where it’s broken down into various other components, most notably coenzyme A esters.14 There is also evidence that gut microflora play a critical role in lysine metabolism before it reaches the liver, but more research is needed to understand this process fully.

Lysine also helps the body synthesize collagen and aids in anxiety reduction by blocking stress response receptors (cortisol) in the body.15 16

When combined with calcium at lower dosages, lysine can prevent and treat osteoporosis by enhancing calcium absorption and improving its renal conservation.17 The enhanced collagen production of lysine assists in healing wounds and increasing cell growth.

Some scientific studies have shown that lysine supplementation can help manage heart disease by preventing lipoprotein from binding to arterial walls, resulting in fewer plaque deposits and pressure buildup in the arteries.18

Lysine’s ability to prevent and treat herpes simplex virus (HSV) I and II by slowing down the body’s production of arginine carries over to treating shingles and other viruses.19 20 It helps to regulate immune cell responses, block apoptosis, and promote cell proliferation.

Who should take a lysine supplement?

Lysine availability far exceeds minimum daily intake levels in North America, making deficiency rare. But pregnant people, those living in food deserts, and people with certain medical conditions may require supplemental lysine.21 There is also bountiful evidence that it can help build muscle for those interested in maximizing their results in the gym.9

Vegetarians and vegans often benefit significantly from lysine supplements because their diets do not contain as many high-protein foods, aside from legumes.22

While much of the evidence linking lysine with renal complications comes from in vitro and animal studies, there is enough of it that anyone with kidney issues might want to have a long conversation with their doctor before trying a lysine supplement.

Should you give lysine to cats?

Based on a small handful of poorly conducted studies, some companies have begun manufacturing lysine supplements for cats to stave off feline herpes infections. More rigorous research says that this is not an effective strategy to manage the ailment and that such supplements are little more than cash grabs preying on the emotions of pet owners.28

Save yourself the time and money, and don’t bother supplementing your cat’s diet with lysine. If further research comes out to verify claims of its efficacy, we’ll certainly amend this article, but the most credible research to date says otherwise.

Are lysine supplements safe?

Lysine supplements are generally well tolerated by healthy adults. However, those with kidney, liver, or gallbladder issues should avoid lysine because it can cause gallstones, renal dysfunction, and failure.23 This evidence is largely based on in vitro and animal studies, however, so your doctor may approve of lysine supplementation after an exam.

Lysine can cause side effects, particularly in higher doses.24 These include:

That said, a large review of studies concluded that 6g/day was a tolerable upper limit for lysine supplementation, which is far beyond what typical supplements recommend in their dosing guidelines.5 And given the rarity of severe lysine deficiency in the U.S., most adults should likely stay at or below 2g/day from supplemental sources.

Frequently asked questions about lysine supplements

How much lysine can I take?

Studies have shown you can take up to 6,000mg of lysine daily without incurring any serious effects.5 However, you may experience adverse gastrointestinal side effects in lower dosages, depending on your sensitivity. These may include nausea, stomach pain, and diarrhea. Other side effects may include kidney issues and gallstones, but these aren’t entirely supported by research. Always consult your doctor before adding a lysine supplement to your routine.

What medications don’t work well with lysine supplements?

If you are taking any ongoing medication or have chronic health issues, you should consult with your doctor before adding lysine or any dietary supplement to your regimen. Some research indicates that lysine may be safe when taken in conjunction with other herpes treatments, but again, this is an issue to discuss with your doctor.27

When should I take lysine supplements?

We recommend taking lysine supplements in the morning with food so that they have the most time to digest. However, you can take them at any time of day, with or without food.

What is the difference between lysine and L-lysine?

Lysine is a primary amino acid and essential nutrient for vertebrates. L-lysine is the biologically active form of lysine that is found in supplements. On its own, lysine isn’t as stable or bioavailable as L-lysine.

Can lysine damage my kidneys?

You should not take lysine supplements if you suffer from kidney issues unless your doctor advises it. In animal studies, researchers have shown that high doses of lysine supplements can cause acute renal failure.7

Do lysine supplements help heal cold sores?

In higher dosages (3g per day), lysine effectively improves cold sores and genital herpes.14 However, you should consult with your doctor before integrating lysine into your daily regimen, especially in high doses.

Can lysine help me lose weight?

Yes, at least if you’re a rat. Animal studies have shown lysine to be an effective tool in managing body weight.1 It won’t cause your pounds to melt away, but it may help augment a multi-faceted approach. Lysine also promotes lean body mass development and muscle strength.

What effects does lysine have on blood pressure?

Lysine supplementation may help lower blood pressure in individuals with hypertension, especially when combined with ornithine and arginine.18 26 However, you should talk with your doctor before adding lysine to your daily regimen, as it can interact with several medications and supplements.

How long will it take until I see results from lysine supplements?

If taking lysine supplements for cold sores or genital herpes, you should see results in one to two days. When taken at lower doses, lysine is likely to affect your energy levels, mood, and weight within several weeks of taking the supplement daily.

Can I get lysine from my diet?

Yes. Many foods contain lysine, particularly protein-rich foods such as meat, poultry, eggs, tofu, cheese, and fish. Lysine supplements are particularly effective for vegetarians and vegans, who obtain less lysine from their daily diets. Most people get enough lysine through their diet alone.

How does lysine affect mental health?

In double-blind, placebo-controlled studies, researchers have shown that when combined with arginine, lysine can reduce chronic anxiety and lower cortisol levels in the body.17

What side effects do lysine supplements have?

The most common side effects of lysine supplementation are gastrointestinal issues (pain, diarrhea, nausea) and an increased risk for gallstones and kidney issues.

What is the lysine contingency?

The lysine contingency was a safety protocol enacted by InGen scientist Dr. Henry Wu in the Jurassic Park novel and films. The idea was that, by tweaking the dinosaurs’ DNA to require more significant amounts of lysine than they could access through their diets, they would be reliant on regular dietary supplementation. If a dinosaur escaped the island, it would succumb to its deficiency and die.

The failure of this contingency is not satisfactorily explained in the films, but the end of the original novel speaks of unknown animals on another island that have been raiding crops rich in lysine, and Dr. Wu laments that the evidence of breeding among the dinosaurs implies their other genetic mechanisms may be faulty.
