What is Amanita muscaria?

Amanita muscaria, or fly agaric, is a psychoactive, poisonous, red or orange-red mushroom with white spots and gills. It has been used for centuries in shamanic and religious rituals, particularly in Asia, Europe, and Siberia.1 2 One of the earliest written references to Amanita muscaria was in the Rigveda, an ancient Indian collection of Sanskrit hymns and the oldest of the sacred books of Hinduism.16 17 This text describes a ritual drink known as soma, believed to grant godlike abilities to those who consumed it. Researchers later linked the euphoric effects of soma to the use of Amanita muscaria.15 16

The popularity of Amanita muscaria persisted well into the Victorian and Edwardian eras.2 It was commonly pictured on Christmas cards as a symbol of good luck, and a popular winter solstice tradition was drying and stringing these mushrooms near the hearth.1

In recent years, mushrooms of all kinds — like lion’s mane, cordyceps, reishi, and chaga — have come into the spotlight as potential wellness ingredients for boosting the immune system and helping the body adapt to stress.18 Because of this, it’s not surprising that Amanita muscaria is beginning to regain popularity, and not just for its psychoactive effects. We’ll delve further into this mushroom’s wellness-boosting potential later, but early animal research appears promising for Amanita muscaria’s ability to help reduce anxiety,19 relieve neuropathic pain, and more.20

Are Amanita muscaria gummies legal in the United States?

Amanita muscaria has historically been marketed under the U.S. Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994 (DSHEA), often categorized alongside other botanical dietary ingredients.28 At the state level, this mushroom remains legal in 49 U.S. states, with the sole exception being Louisiana.21

If you live in Louisiana, you’ll need to consider an alternative. CBD products — such as oils and gummies — may offer some overlapping benefits and are widely available. (We break down our top CBD picks in a separate guide.)

At the same time, federal regulatory scrutiny of Amanita muscaria has increased in recent years.

FDA position on Amanita muscaria

On December 18, 2024, the FDA issued a warning letter to food manufacturers stating that Amanita muscaria (including its extracts and constituents, such as muscimol and ibotenic acid) is “not authorized” for use in conventional foods due to safety concerns around its “pharmacologically active constituents.”57 58 59

More recently, in September 2025, the FDA issued an additional warning letter to Blue Forest Farms, LLC, stating that dietary supplements containing Amanita muscaria are considered adulterated under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. The agency asserted that Amanita muscaria and its constituents qualify as “new dietary ingredients” for which no adequate safety notification has been submitted. The FDA also stated that Amanita muscaria is not generally recognized as safe (GRAS) for use in conventional foods and that food products containing it may be considered adulterated under federal law.13

It’s worth noting that being excluded from GRAS status does not automatically remove a substance from the market. Other ingredients that have faced similar regulatory scrutiny or lack GRAS status include CBD, THC, melatonin, and Ginkgo biloba,60 yet products containing those ingredients remain widely available in the U.S. That said, the FDA’s recent warning letters suggest that Amanita muscaria may be subject to closer oversight than it was in previous years.

What makes up Amanita muscaria?

The active components of raw Amanita muscaria mushrooms you’re most likely to hear about are two of the psychoactive alkaloids: muscimol and ibotenic acid. However, there’s more to be found in these mushrooms, including other chemical and bioactive compounds.

All of these compounds are considered toxic when consumed via raw Amanita muscaria mushroom.3 For this reason, traditional preparation methods — and modern extraction processes — aim to reduce ibotenic acid levels while concentrating muscimol. Commercial gummies use processed extracts rather than raw mushroom material, and reputable manufacturers provide third-party lab documentation to support their safety practices. As discussed earlier, the depth and scope of that testing can vary between brands.

Below, we outline the primary compounds found in raw Amanita muscaria and what researchers know about them.

An important note: The following information refers to the chemistry of the raw mushroom.14 Properly manufactured extracts used in gummies are processed to reduce undesirable compounds and are not equivalent to consuming raw mushroom material.41

Muscarine

This toxic compound is a selective cholinergic agonist that can produce some, but not all, of the same parasympathetic effects as acetylcholine.3 29 Some of these effects include slowing the heart, lowering blood pressure, and increasing the secretion of bodily fluids (such as saliva, tears, and gastric acid).22

A class of drugs called “muscarinic agonists” can also lead to similar effects and are sometimes used as a treatment for urinary retention, glaucoma, Alzheimer’s disease, or other conditions where it may be beneficial to increase smooth muscle tone. These drugs don’t necessarily contain muscarine but instead bind to and activate muscarinic cholinergic receptors.22

Ibotenic acid

Ibotenic acid, muscimol, and muscazone all fall under the umbrella of the “isoxazoles” (a type of compound with oxygen and nitrogen atoms in specific positions) found in Amanita muscaria. Some research suggests that products containing derivatives of isoxazoles may have anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, anti-Alzheimer’s, antioxidant, antifungal,30 immunoregulatory, and antidiabetic properties, among others.31

However, ibotenic acid is a neurotoxin that has been utilized for years in animal-model research studies as a “potent brain-lesioning agent” (i.e., a substance that damages tissue in the brain) to study cognitive dysfunction in neurodegenerative diseases,32 Alzheimer’s disease,3 strokes, and traumatic brain injuries (TBIs).33 When subjected to certain chemical changes, ibotenic acid becomes the less-toxic muscazone (via UV-radiation-assisted isomerization) and the relatively nontoxic muscimol (via decarboxylation).34 35

Muscazone

As noted above, muscazone is produced when ibotenic acid is broken down via UV radiation.3 It’s “less toxic” than ibotenic acid, and it doesn’t have much pharmacological activity compared to the other components of Amanita muscaria,35 but some researchers have found that it, for an as-yet-unknown reason, has a longer-lasting effect on the body and actually “reinforces” the toxicity of the other compounds. It can lead to memory loss, attention issues, disorientation, and partial vision loss.34

Muscimol

In Amanita muscaria, muscimol and ibotenic acid are considered to be the main psychoactive substances,3 and this may be why a lot of research paints the former to be nearly as dangerous as the latter.36 Though it’s being investigated for its benefits, muscimol is still technically a toxin and isn’t without its risks. For instance, it can be dangerous when consumed in excessive amounts or when the pure chemical form is inhaled.37

While some Amanita muscaria gummies containing extracts of the mushroom may have extremely small traces of some of these compounds (like ibotenic acid), the levels are nowhere near what you’d consume if you ate the actual mushroom. For example, the lab reports from Utoya list ibotenic acid as “ND,” or “not detected,” even though it utilizes Amanita muscaria extract.

And even if it did happen to contain trace amounts of this compound, the psychoactive effects of ibotenic acid generally don’t appear until doses reach roughly 30–60mg.38 39 Additionally, ibotenic acid is rapidly converted into muscimol after ingestion through a process called decarboxylation, meaning trace amounts would likely end up contributing to muscimol levels rather than remaining as ibotenic acid.7

Interact with or alter the effectiveness of cholinergic and anticholinergic drugs22

Not be suitable for those taking medications for high blood pressure, glaucoma, Alzheimer’s disease, or urinary retention22

Potentially be dangerous for those with epilepsy due to muscimol’s effect on GABA23 24

Lead to digestive upset, such as mild nausea or diarrhea7

Cause visual, auditory, and other perceptual changes in high doses7

Some, but not all, of the antioxidants in Amanita muscaria are ergothioneine, glutathione, and ascorbic acid (vitamin C), along with various lignans and flavonoids.

Amanita muscaria gummies FAQ

Are Amanita muscaria gummies safe?

Amanita muscaria gummies are generally made using curated extracts that don’t contain dangerous amounts of any toxins. To ensure you’re purchasing a safe product, we recommend checking to see if the company subjects its products to third-party testing and is transparent about the results (like our top picks).

Is Amanita muscaria the same as magic mushrooms?

No. Amanita muscaria and “magic mushrooms,” or “shrooms,” aren’t the same thing. While Amanita muscaria can be psychoactive, it doesn’t contain psilocybin — the primary hallucinogen in shrooms.

Can you get addicted to Amanita muscaria?

Technically, it’s possible to become dependent on or addicted to pretty much anything from work, the internet, and shopping to gambling, alcohol, smoking, and drugs.55 However, because of their unpleasant side effects (e.g., nausea, headaches), the National Institute on Drug Abuse (NIDA) explains that psychedelic drugs, like psilocybin and LSD, don’t typically lead to addiction. Unfortunately, some people may quickly form a tolerance to these substances, leading to needing more for the same effects.56

The information from NIDA doesn’t specifically mention Amanita muscaria, but psilocybin is the next-closest substance that has adequate research available. We’ll update this FAQ as more information is found over time.

Are Amanita muscaria gummies legal to purchase?

Amanita muscaria gummies should be legal to purchase in 49 U.S. states. The one state that doesn’t allow it is Louisiana. In America, these mushrooms, or “botanicals,” currently fall under the Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994 (DSHEA), which means they can be sold without FDA approval.21 28 In December 2024, the FDA determined Amanita muscaria to be “not GRAS” or not generally recognized as safe. For context, other not-GRAS ingredients include CBD, THC, melatonin, and Ginkgo biloba — all of which can still be found in products sold today.60

That said, the regulatory landscape is evolving, and future federal or state actions could affect how these products are sold or distributed.

How do Amanita muscaria gummies taste?

Many Amanita muscaria gummies lean toward earthy or slightly bitter notes. If you’ve tried CBD gummies, the flavor profile is fairly similar. They aren’t unpleasant, but they’re also not candy-like in the way that you’d eat them just for the taste. In our testers’ experience, Utoya stood out as the most enjoyable overall, with the cleanest finish and the least noticeable aftertaste.

How long does it take Amanita muscaria gummies to work?

It varies from person to person, but Amanita muscaria gummies could take anywhere from 30 minutes to two hours to kick in. We recommend waiting at least an hour after taking one before deciding to take more. The effects may last up to around six hours for some people.
