Important Medical Info: Why a Doctor Checks for Infection Post-Cleanse

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Colon hydrotherapy carries risks including microscopic tears in the colon wall and bacterial translocation into the bloodstream, which can lead to serious infection or sepsis. After the procedure, doctors typically monitor vital signs, order blood work, and may use imaging to detect early signs of infection. Key risk factors include pre-existing bowel conditions, recent abdominal surgery, and weakened immunity. Warning signs requiring immediate medical attention include fever, severe abdominal pain, rectal bleeding, rapid heartbeat, and dizziness.

Explanation

Colon hydrotherapy (also known as colonic irrigation) is a procedure in which filtered water is gently infused into the large intestine to remove waste material. While many people consider it a safe way to promote digestive health, any time the lining of the bowel is disturbed there is a small chance bacteria can enter the bloodstream. When this happens, the body's immune system reacts—and in some cases this can lead to a serious condition called sepsis.

Below, we explain in clear terms why your doctor will be on the lookout for infection after a colon cleanse, what sepsis risk factors are involved, what signs to watch for, and when to seek medical help.

Why Infection Monitoring Matters

Even when performed by a trained professional under sterile conditions, colon hydrotherapy can:

Cause microscopic tears or irritation of the intestinal mucosa

Alter the balance of normal gut bacteria

Introduce waterborne bacteria or contaminants if equipment is not perfectly sterile

These effects are generally mild and self-limited. However, if harmful bacteria penetrate the intestinal wall and enter your bloodstream, your body may mount an exaggerated inflammatory response. This can progress to sepsis, which is life-threatening if not recognized and treated promptly.

Understanding Sepsis Risk

Sepsis is not a single infection but rather the body's extreme reaction to an infection. Key points include:

It can develop within hours or days of initial bacterial exposure.

Early symptoms are often vague (fever, chills, fatigue) but can escalate rapidly.

If untreated, sepsis can progress to septic shock—dangerously low blood pressure and organ failure.

Risk factors that increase sepsis risk after a cleanse:

Weakened immune system (e.g., diabetes, chronic steroid use)

Existing illness or infection elsewhere in the body

Older age or very young age

Recent surgeries or invasive procedures

Your doctor checks for signs of infection to catch any early warning signs before they turn into full-blown sepsis.

Common Signs and Symptoms to Watch

After a colon hydrotherapy session, keep an eye on how you feel. Important signs include:

Unexplained fever (temperature above 100.4°F or 38°C)

Rapid heart rate (tachycardia)

Fast, shallow breathing

Confusion, dizziness, or feeling unusually weak

Persistent abdominal pain or cramping

Redness, swelling, or discharge if any catheter sites were used

If you notice any of these, let your healthcare provider know right away. Early detection is key to preventing complications.

What Your Doctor Will Do

When you return for a post-cleanse checkup, your doctor may:

Take a thorough history of any new symptoms

Perform a physical exam, focusing on vital signs (temperature, pulse, blood pressure)

Order blood tests (complete blood count, C-reactive protein, lactic acid)

Possibly take blood cultures to identify bacteria in the bloodstream

Recommend imaging (ultrasound or CT scan) if an intra-abdominal source is suspected

These steps help determine whether an infection is present and whether sepsis risk is rising.

How to Respond if You Suspect an Infection

If you experience any concerning signs after your cleanse, take these steps:

Contact your doctor immediately; do not wait for a scheduled appointment.

If you cannot reach your provider and symptoms are severe (high fever, rapid breathing, extreme weakness), go to the nearest emergency department or call emergency services.

When to Speak to a Doctor

Always take post-procedure symptoms seriously. No matter how minor they seem, if you have:

Severe or persistent fever

Confusion, extreme fatigue, or feeling faint

Pain that does not improve with rest or over-the-counter pain relief

Signs of dehydration (dizziness, decreased urination)

—speak to a doctor right away. If you ever feel you are experiencing something life-threatening or rapidly worsening, go to the emergency department.
