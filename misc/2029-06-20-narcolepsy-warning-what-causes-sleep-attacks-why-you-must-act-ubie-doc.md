Internal Medicine

Narcolepsy is a chronic neurological sleep disorder that causes excessive daytime sleepiness and sudden "sleep attacks," where a person falls asleep uncontrollably. It's believed to be triggered by the immune system mistakenly attacking brain cells that produce hypocretin, a chemical that regulates wakefulness. Common symptoms include cataplexy (sudden muscle weakness), sleep paralysis, and vivid hallucinations.

Because narcolepsy shares symptoms with many other conditions, identifying it early is key to getting the right care. Narcolepsy is a condition where people suddenly fall asleep during the day. This happens because the brain has trouble controlling sleep and wakefulness. One reason for narcolepsy is that the immune system might mistakenly attack parts of the brain that help us stay awake.

Causes of Narcolepsy:

Immune System Attack: Research suggests that narcolepsy, especially Type 1, may occur when the immune system attacks brain cells that produce a chemical called hypocretin, which helps keep us awake.
