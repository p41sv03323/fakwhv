Understanding infertility: What causes it?

Infertility is the inability to conceive despite unprotected sex for over a year. About 48 million couples globally experience this reproductive disorder.

Nearly 45% of reported infertility issues affect both males and females. Scientists predict an even bleaker future due to evolving lifestyle habits.

This problem can arise from various physical, emotional, and sociocultural issues. Oxidative stress (OS) and conditions like varicocele, cryptorchidism, and hypogonadism also cause infertility. In rare cases, certain genetic factors trigger this reproductive disorder.

How do you salvage your reproductive health? Common remedies include surgical extraction of defective tissue or hormonal fertility supplements. The latter has promising results when administered through antioxidants, which reduce oxidative damage.

What’s the role of oxidative stress in infertility?

Oxidative stress is an imbalance of defensive antioxidants and reactive oxygen species (ROS). Simply put, excessive ROS levels or reduced antioxidant defenses result in OS.

This anomaly influences most of the male and female reproductive attributes throughout their lifetime.

Current scientific research shows OS can contribute to male infertility through:

Lowering sperm motility

Damaging sperm DNA

Increasing the risk of genetic diseases and perennial abortions

Besides male fertility, OS can affect pregnancy and a newborn’s genetic status. Treatment with antioxidants can boost fertility and reduce disease transmission to your offspring.

Scientific evidence shows low antioxidant reserves in the body accelerate OS. Antioxidants like vitamins A, C, and E protect you from ROS. They help neutralize its production and adverse effects.

ROS are highly reactive compounds containing oxygen. They can damage biomolecules and injure body cells and tissue.

The body naturally produces some ROS as a normal biological process. Harm can only occur when it releases excessive ROS.

How do antioxidants work against ROS? They have three modes of action:

Directly hunting for ROS already established in the system

Inhibiting the formation of ROS at the source

Repairing or removing the damage or stress already caused by ROS
