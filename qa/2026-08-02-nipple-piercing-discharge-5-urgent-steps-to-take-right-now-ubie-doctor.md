What should I do if I notice discharge from my nipple piercing?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Nipple piercing discharge is often a sign of infection, irritation, or delayed healing. Common causes include bacterial infection, allergic reaction to jewelry metals, poor aftercare, or trauma to the site. Discharge may appear yellow, green, or white, and can be accompanied by redness, swelling, pain, warmth, or a foul odor.

What to do: Keep the area clean with a saline solution, avoid touching the piercing with unwashed hands, and do not remove the jewelry (this can trap infection). Seek medical care if discharge persists, worsens, or comes with fever or spreading redness.
