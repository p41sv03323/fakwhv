What should I do if Tylenol isn’t working and I keep needing more?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

When Tylenol (acetaminophen) doesn't relieve your pain, it's a signal to consult a doctor rather than take more. Exceeding the recommended dose—generally 3,000–4,000 mg per day for adults—can cause serious liver damage, even in otherwise healthy people. Persistent pain that doesn't respond to standard doses may also point to an underlying issue that needs proper evaluation, not stronger self-medication.

Common reasons Tylenol may not work include:

Pain caused by inflammation (where NSAIDs like ibuprofen may work better)

Nerve-related pain, which often requires different treatments
