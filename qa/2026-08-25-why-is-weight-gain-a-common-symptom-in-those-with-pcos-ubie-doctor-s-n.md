Internal Medicine

Weight gain in PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome) is primarily driven by hormonal imbalances, particularly insulin resistance and elevated androgens, which slow metabolism and promote fat storage—especially around the abdomen. These hormonal shifts can make weight harder to lose through diet and exercise alone, and unexplained weight gain is often one of the earliest signs of PCOS.

If you're noticing changes in your weight, energy, or menstrual cycle, don't guess—get clarity. Understanding whether hormones are behind your symptoms is the first step toward effective treatment, and early identification of PCOS can help prevent long-term complications like type 2 diabetes and heart disease. Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) often leads to weight gain. Patients with PCOS often have insulin resistance and increased rates of metabolic syndrome. Key Points:

Hormonal Imbalance: PCOS causes an imbalance in hormones, such as increased insulin resistance

Metabolic Factors: According to research, metabolic determinants play a significant role in weight gain, and these factors can be influenced by PCOS.

Age and Lifestyle: As women age, especially during midlife, weight gain can become more pronounced due to changes in metabolism and lifestyle, as noted in the literature.

Understanding these factors can help manage weight and improve overall health for those with PCOS.
