What is an iron infusion?

An iron infusion is a procedure where iron is introduced intravenously to boost iron levels in the body. Iron infusions are typically recommended for people who are suffering from iron-deficiency anemia. The most common prescription to treat iron-deficiency anemia is iron pills, but the doctor can sometimes prescribe iron infusions if the patient’s iron levels are extremely low because it is a faster alternative than taking iron supplements.

Iron infusions are also recommended for patients who cannot ingest iron orally because they have underlying digestive issues. Patients who cannot absorb iron due to blood loss and need to restore their iron levels faster are also prime candidates for an iron infusion.

What to expect during an in-home iron infusion?

Iron infusions that are performed in-home carry the same process as any standard IV treatments. The main difference is that the nurse or medical professional will also perform a test dose to ensure that the patient will not have any allergic reaction to the iron. The typical timeframe for iron infusion sessions is 3-4 hours.

The process starts with introducing the needle that is required to insert a small tube into the vein. This small tube is the vessel where iron will be introduced to the bloodstream. This small tube is known as a catheter and is usually attached to the hand or arm. Once inserted, the needle will be removed, which leaves the catheter in place in the vein.

The catheter is attached to the long tube that is connected to an IV bag containing the iron. The iron in the IV bag is typically diluted in a saline solution. The actual process itself is safe and painless. You might feel a small pinch when the needle breaks the skin and a little pressure when the infusion starts.

The slow infusion rate is purposely done to avoid any complications and minimize the side effects.

Why would a patient need an iron infusion?

Patients typically need an iron infusion to correct low blood iron levels, although infusions may also be given in advance of a medical procedure. There are many potential reasons a patient might have low blood iron, including illness, certain medical conditions, side effects from other treatments, inability to absorb adequate iron from diet or supplements, and more.

What happens during an iron infusion?

A nurse will arrive at your location at your appointment time with the necessary equipment for your infusion. They will then administer a test dose via a needle and catheter to ensure that you do not have an allergic reaction to the ingredients in the infusion.

After your test dose, your infusion will begin. This process typically takes several hours. Following your infusion, you can resume activities as normal unless otherwise instructed.

How to prepare for an iron infusion

The most significant preparation required for an iron infusion is to ensure you set aside enough time for treatment. Unlike some procedures, there is no need to fast prior to this procedure.

Iron infusions may take 3 or 4 hours, so bring a book, a game, music or podcasts, a computer, or sit near your TV so you can stay occupied during treatment.

Eat breakfast, stay hydrated, and take your medication as normal unless otherwise instructed by your doctor.

Wear comfortable, loose-fitting clothing. You may also want to consider having a blanket on hand if you get cold easily.
