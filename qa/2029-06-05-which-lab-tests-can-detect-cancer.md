Genetic testing is the process of using diagnostic procedures to search for specific gene alterations, or mutations. There are now many different genetic test types in use, and more are being created.

When a Genetic Cancer Lab Test Should Be Done?

One sort of testing used to search for inherited DNA alterations that may increase an individual’s chance of developing a particular type of cancer is called predictive genetic testing. It may be recommended to do this kind of testing for:

To determine whether they have a gene mutation that raises their risk, a person with a strong family history of a particular form of cancer should be examined. Should they possess an inherited mutation, they may choose to undergo screening tests to detect cancer at an early stage or even try to reduce their risk. One example would be screening individuals with several family members with a history of breast cancer for alterations in the BRCA1 and BRCA2 genes, which are known to raise the risk of breast cancer and certain other malignancies.

An individual who has already received a cancer diagnosis, particularly if there are further indications that the disease may have been brought on by an inherited mutation (strong family history, early diagnosis, or rare disease, such breast cancer in a male).
